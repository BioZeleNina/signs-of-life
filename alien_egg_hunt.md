# The alien egg hunt

*An optional extra mission, available in sessions 9 and 10 or at home.*

---

## What this is

Five references to science fiction works are embedded in the course
materials. They appear in the mission briefing, in ARIADNE-7's
dialogue, in file names, and in the session text itself.

Finding all five and submitting the correct confirmation phrases
unlocks a hidden file: the classified backstory of what actually
happened to the two previous survey missions that declared this planet
lifeless. You share the final line of that file with the course
instructor to confirm you found them all.

This does not affect your grade. There is no time limit.

---

## Rules

- You may use any resources: the course materials, the internet,
  friends, books.
- You may not ask ARIADNE-7 directly. She knows the answers.
  She will not tell you. She considers this a matter of principle.
- Each signal has a confirmation phrase. You need all five.
- Submit with: `ariadne easter_egg submit`

---

## The five signals

**Signal 1 -- The highway**

Read the mission briefing carefully. Why was this planet marked for
clearance in the Galactic Survey Registry? The reason is not
scientific. It is infrastructural. And it is a direct reference to
one of the most beloved works of British science fiction comedy.

*What is being constructed that required this planet to be cleared?*

---

**Signal 2 -- The computer's predecessor**

ARIADNE-7 did not invent her conversational style. She borrowed it.
Somewhere in her self-description and dialogue is a clear nod to an
earlier fictional AI -- British, sardonic, technically omniscient,
and somehow always slightly wrong. If you know the show, you will
recognise her immediately.

*Name the AI and the show.*

---

**Signal 3 -- Resistance**

The very last sentence of the prologue does not belong to the course
designer. It belongs to someone else entirely -- someone in a
franchise that also contributed the opening epigraph. Look at it
carefully. It is not original advice.

*What is the phrase, and where does it come from?*

---

**Signal 4 -- The twice-dead world**

ARIADNE-7's mission log contains a sentence about failure and
frameworks. That sentence is not hers originally either. It
describes the central problem of a classic science fiction novel --
one about a planet where scientists fail to understand what they are
looking at, not because they lack intelligence, but because they lack
the right category of thought.

The novel is set on a planet covered entirely by a single vast ocean.
The ocean is alive. The scientists cannot decide what to make of it.
The novel was written in the 1960s by a European author who
is considered one of the greatest science fiction writers of the
twentieth century.

*Name the novel and its author.*

---

**Signal 5 -- Nonlinear orthography**

In session 2, ARIADNE-7 uses a specific technical phrase to describe
the alien writing system. That phrase comes from somewhere. Follow
it. It connects to a story about language, time, and what happens
when you learn to think in a structure your brain was not built for.

In the source work, the alien writing system uses circular, radial
symbols where meaning is encoded in the whole shape at once -- not
read left to right, but perceived all at once. A linguist learns to
write this way. It changes how she experiences time. The story was
first published as a short story, then adapted into a film.

*Name the work the phrase comes from.*

## How to submit

Inside your Docker container:

```bash
ariadne easter_egg submit
```

ARIADNE-7 will prompt for your five confirmation phrases one at a time.
Get all five correct and she will unlock the classified archive.

---

## The reward

If all five signals are confirmed, ARIADNE-7 unlocks a hidden file
containing the classified backstory of Survey Missions 1 and 2.

The final line of that file is what you share with the course
instructor to confirm you found all five. Post it to the course forum
or email it directly.

---

## A note on the eggs

Whoever designed this course believes science fiction and science are
in conversation with each other, and that this conversation is worth
noticing. The five works referenced here each engage, in different
ways, with the problem of encountering something genuinely unknown --
and with the ways our frameworks for understanding the world can
prevent us from seeing what is actually there.

That is also what the course is about.
